Kimono Rental in Japan: 6 Real Shops for Tourists

A researched, honest roundup of kimono and costume rental shops in Kyoto, Tokyo/Asakusa and Osaka — with real prices and what each one is actually good at.

Walking through a place like Kyoto's Gion district or Asakusa in a kimono is one of those things that sounds like a tourist cliché — until you actually do it, and it turns your ordinary sightseeing photos into something you'll keep forever. Renting is easy: most shops dress you in about 20–30 minutes, no reservation-day commitment beyond a few hours, and staff are used to first-timers who've never worn one before.

Riri's note I haven't worn all of these myself yet — this list is built from real, currently operating shops and their official pricing, cross-checked across sources. I'll add my own visits as I go, and mark clearly if any link ever becomes a paid partnership.

Kyoto

Yumeyakata

Gion / near Gojo Station  ·  from ¥3,080 (couple sets around ¥8,000, furisode from ¥16,500)

The widest size range on this list — kids from 85cm up to plus-size (4LW) and tall sizes for men up to 195cm. Multilingual staff, an 8-style hair-arrangement add-on, and optional tea-ceremony or photo add-ons if you want to make a whole afternoon of it.

Kyoto Station store (also Asakusa, Kamakura, Nagoya, Kanazawa, Fukuoka, Sendai)  ·  from ¥3,000–3,500, deluxe plans available

The one to pick if you want a sure bet for English support — plus Mandarin and Korean. There's an optional damage-insurance add-on, which is a nice bit of peace of mind if you've never handled a kimono before.

A few things worth knowing before you book

Good to know

  • Most shops dress you in 20–30 minutes and don't require booking days ahead in the off-season
  • Hair styling is sometimes included, sometimes a small add-on — check before comparing prices
  • Several shops offer free luggage storage or hotel returns, handy if you're sightseeing all day

Worth planning around

  • Peak cherry-blossom and autumn-foliage weeks get busy — same-day walk-ins can mean a wait
  • Walking distance in geta sandals takes some getting used to; don't plan a huge walking day on top
